LA 23 — mapped roof colours

The DB was re-imported on 2026-10-16 with roof:colour and building:colour (added to osm_import.lua). In the Schillerkiez, 455 of 6014 buildings have roof:colour and 233 have building:colour, mostly as #rrggbb.

A first, literal mapping broke the mission (left column of roofs.jpg). building:colour turned the Herrfurthplatz church fully red. roof:colour #4A5624 turned whole courtyard blocks sage. Grey tags pulled the white volumes towards grey.

The rule now is that mapped data decides where the accents go, within the plan's vocabulary:

  • Only roof:colour is used. building:colour is the facade colour, which a plan view does not show.
  • Red: saturated red, orange or brown, with saturation (max − min) / max ≥ 0.3. Gets the terracotta accent only on small non-landmark buildings (< 600 m²).
  • Green: a saturated green roof gets a very pale sage tint #E6E9DD.
  • Plain: every other parsable value (greys, whites, khaki #948975, taupe #A29893, blues) renders as a white volume, and such a building is never picked for the random red accent.
  • Untagged buildings keep the old rules.

Unit test roof_tone_classes in style_la_extra.rs.

Look at: roofs.jpg (top: Herrfurthplatz, bottom: courtyard blocks in scene D; left: literal mapping, right: now).

LA 21 — street-tree rows

Tree vocabulary item 3 from the mission: *street tree row — same symbol, more uniform in colour.* Before, every street tree picked symbol and tone from its own id, so a row on Allerstraße mixed three symbols and six tones.

  • The tree query now also returns the name of the nearest named road within ~12 m (highway, type = 'road', KNN on the GiST index).
  • A tree without genus that stands near a street and not inside lawn/park or woodland takes symbol and tone from a hash of the street name, with a small per-tree lightness jitter so the row is uniform but not stamped. Default crown 5.5 m when diameter_crown is missing.
  • Park trees keep the varied generic symbols; trees with a genus keep their genus symbol.

LA 19 — bridges, genus trees, play and furniture symbols

Ink 25 — path junctions

Park and footway paths are drawn as double lines (a wide ink casing, then a narrower paper fill). Until now each path was drawn in one go, casing then fill, so at a meeting point the round cap of one path's casing sat on top of the other path's paper. Every junction showed a pill-shaped overlap instead of a clean meeting of lines.

What changed

  • Two passes over all paths (style_ink.rs, the paths block): first every casing, then every paper fill. A path's paper now erases the casing of each path it meets, so junctions read as one plan drawing: the lines run into each other and stop at the open side.
  • Casings in their own layer: the casings are drawn into a transparent layer and composited once at the ink alpha (0.75). Drawn path by path, the translucent ink would darken where two casings overlap at a junction.
  • Paper is one colour, so the fill order (by width) makes no visible difference and needs no grouping.
  • Sidewalks and crossings are still skipped. Paths that end on a carriageway or a plaza still open into it: their paper erases the outline where they meet.

Look at

  • junctions-before-after.jpg: the Tempelhofer Feld / Hasenheide junction cluster at z19, before (left) and after (right). The pill caps are gone.
  • before-after.jpg: this step against step 24 (scenes A–D). Only the path junctions differ.

  • seam_check.sh ink: 0.001 % of pixels differ (limit 0.1 %).

Ink 24 — one light, from the north-west

Until now the ink style had two lights. Roofs from the facets (step 22) and the landscape-architecture style used light from the north-west (azimuth 315°). The older ink shading still assumed south-west: the cross-hatched half of per-building roofs was the NE half, and the shadows of lifted buildings fell east-north-east. Both now follow the same light as the facets.

What changed

  • Per-building roofs (flat or unroofed buildings, and every lifted building): the cross-hatched shaded half is the side facing the shadow direction, south-east. It was north-east. The facets already hatched the SE/E/S-facing planes, so both kinds of roof now shade alike.
  • Lifted buildings (SRK_INK_OBLIQUE=freestanding): the hatched ground shadow falls south-east, the direction of the light's shadow, instead of east-north-east.
  • Bridge parapets (style_ink_extra.rs): the hatched shadow strokes under a south parapet lean south-east.
  • One source for the light: roof_shapes::LIGHT_AZIMUTH_DEG = 315°, with lit_factor (facets) and shadow_dir_px (pixel direction of cast shadows, south-east). Every ink shading direction is derived from these. A unit test checks that a facet is in shade exactly when it faces the shadow direction, and that this agrees with lit_factor.

Checked and already consistent (unchanged)

  • Tree crowns: shading strokes on the lower right (SE side).
  • Woodland crowns: the arc and shading strokes on the lower side of each crown.
  • Spire, clock tower and bell turret: their shading (ticks, right-hand half) is on the east face, which faces away from the NW light.
  • Roof facets (draw_roof_facets): already NW.
  • Facade bands (plan south bands and the south walls of lifted buildings): a uniform paper tint with window ticks. They carry no direction, so they are unchanged.

Look at

  • before-after.jpg: the step before (22) against this step. Most of the map is identical. The difference is confined to the shaded side of per-building roofs and to the shadows of lifted buildings.
  • sheet-obl.jpg: scenes A–D with SRK_INK_OBLIQUE=freestanding. The lifted groups now cast their shadow south-east, below and to the right of the building.

Checks

  • cargo test --release: green (21 tests + the new shade_follows_the_shadow_direction).
  • seam_check.sh ink: 0.002 % mean, worst tile 0.01 %. With SRK_INK_OBLIQUE=freestanding: 0.002 % mean, worst 0.02 %.
  • Clippy: no new warnings in the touched files; the existing ones remain.

Ink 22 — roof shapes from processing

Roofs now come from data/processing/sql/roof_shape.sql (branch design-roof-shapes, see ROOF-SHAPES.md): touching buildings of the same roof kind are merged into one roof mass, hipped masses get their planes from the straight skeleton, gabled ones from a centreline split.

  • Plan-view buildings that belong to a sloped mass lose their per-building slope hatch and ridge. Instead every facet is hatched along its ridge (perpendicular to the downslope bearing), facets facing away from the NW light get a second hatch (cross-hatch), and ridges (heavy) and hips (lighter) are drawn on top.
  • The hatch is clipped to each facet analytically (hatch_in_polygon in style_kit.rs) — no mask per facet, world-anchored like all other hatches.
  • Two passes: plan-view buildings, then the facets, then lifted (oblique variant) buildings on top. Flat roofs keep the old single hatch.
  • From z17; SRK_ROOFS=off switches back. Without the roof tables (e.g. a DB imported before this step) the old drawing is used automatically.

Look at: before-after.jpg — perimeter blocks now read as one continuous hipped roof with hips at the corners instead of strips per house. Known issue: small bowtie facets where narrow courtyard wings meet (see ROOF-SHAPES.md, next steps).

Ink 11 — variant: oblique view for free-standing buildings

The open question of the exploration — *where pseudo-3D, where plan?* — as a switch:

``bash SRK_INK_OBLIQUE=freestanding srk-render --layers ink … ``

  • A building is free-standing when no other building (not counting building:part) lies within 0.8 m (SQL NOT EXISTS … ST_DWithin).
  • Free-standing buildings and landmarks are lifted: the roof (with hatch, ridge, shaded half) moves north by a stylised height (½ of height / building:levels×3 m, clamped 3–18 m; landmarks 0.6×), the south walls are drawn from the footprint up to the roof with one row of windows per ~3 m. The footprint stays correct on the ground — the convention of bird's-eye city views.
  • Rows and perimeter blocks stay in plan view, so dense streets never collide.
  • From z17; painted north → south like everything upright.

Look at: compare-c.jpg (allotment huts at Hasenheide: plan left, oblique right) and compare-a.jpg (Genezarethkirche). sheet.jpg shows the four scenes with the variant on.

Verdict (Claude): this reads most like the "Step 8 – ink your village" reference wherever buildings stand alone (parks, allotments, schools, the church) and costs nothing in dense blocks. Candidate for the default.

Ink 06 — roof volume without 3D
  • Where a building has a ridge (roughly rectangular footprint), the roof half facing away from the light (north-east) gets a second hatch parallel to the ridge → cross-hatching = shade. The lit half keeps the single slope hatch.
  • Result: rows of houses read as volumes, close to the "Step 8 – ink your village" reference, while staying a plan.

Look at: detail-b.jpg (courtyard block at Schillerpromenade).

Ink 05 — street names and a switchable accent
  • Street names from label_highway, drawn with resvg textPath in *Hoefler Text Italic* with a paper halo; placed at world-stable positions (middle of the full street line, repeated every ~560 px), so neighbouring tiles agree.
  • Accent category is a render parameter: SRK_ACCENT=playground (default) | bench | tree | none. Everything else stays ink. Benches are now small upright glyphs (z18+, or always when they are the accent).

``bash SRK_ACCENT=bench srk-render --layers ink … ``

Look at: accent-playground.jpg vs accent-bench.jpg (same area), accent-tree.jpg, labels in sheet.jpg.

Ink 01 — first pass in Rust (`srk-render --layers ink`)

First rendering of the fantasy ink style directly from PostGIS, in rust/srk-render/src/style_ink.rs.

Decision for houses (Tobias: "muss nicht 3D sein"): plan view with a hint of volume — roof hatched along the slope (perpendicular to the long axis of ST_OrientedEnvelope), ridge line where the footprint is roughly rectangular, and a narrow south facade band with window ticks on every south-facing edge. Only landmarks (church) and trees stand up. Everything upright is painted north → south.

  • Ground: carriageway outline + sparse stipple, park paths as double lines, lawn tufts, shrub loops, sand stipple, water with shore ripples and wave strokes.
  • Accent: playgrounds in vermilion (outline, 45° hatch, light wash, equipment glyphs) — the rest stays ink.
  • All noise and scatter is world-anchored, so tiles join seamlessly.

Look at: the woodland in scene C; the playgrounds in B and D; facade bands along the blocks. To fix: the church gets two spires (one per church polygon), lawn tufts read as dots at z19.
